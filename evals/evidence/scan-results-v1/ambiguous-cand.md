## Call guide (25 min)

**1. Opening (2 min)**
"The scan is a read-only look at your own ATS data as of 29 October. It is not a judgement on how you work; it shows where work is waiting." Then the one number: "It projects between 6 and 11.7 hours a week that your 9 consultants could get back, starting with work that is already in the system."

**2. Their database today (5 min)**
- 1,143 candidates, 994 added in the last year: a young, active database. 114 placements by 9 consultants, about 13 each.
- "How many people can we actually talk to?" Reachability 55%: roughly one in two candidates has a usable phone or email. Completeness 37.2%: most profiles are thin.
- Consent recorded for 46.3%. Say plainly: this caps any outreach automation until it is fixed, and fixing it is itself an automation.
- Dormant 5.7%: almost nobody has been forgotten yet. Good news, and the reason there is no reactivation upside today.

**3. Three opportunities (10 min)** — conservative figure first, both labelled as projections.

**A. 6,095 matches without a candidate response**
Use case: **Matching follow-up nudge** (WhatsApp, email fallback).
Starts: a match has had no candidate reply after a set number of days. Does: sends one personal message asking if they are still interested and available. Writes: the answer and new availability on the match and candidate. You get: procedures that move or close instead of stalling.
Projection: about 23 min/wk (scan figure; no conservative split given).
Test question: "What happens today when a candidate doesn't react to a match? Who chases, and after how long?"

**B. 1,155 companies without an account manager**
Use case: **Ownership and distribution cleanup** (internal data-hygiene step), which then unlocks **Client reactivation** in the account manager's name.
Starts: a company record has no owner. Does: assigns by rule (last placement, last contact, office). Writes: owner on the company. You get: a client list someone is accountable for, and a working base for client reactivation.
Projection: about 1.5 hrs/wk (scan figure).
Test question: "If one of these 1,155 companies called tomorrow with a role, who would take it?"

**C. 723 new candidates not yet screened**
Use case: **Pre-screening** (WhatsApp).
Starts: a new candidate enters the ATS. Does: sends a short screening form (availability, role, location, salary band). Writes: answers to the profile, raising completeness. You get: screened candidates before a consultant spends time, and better profiles.
Projection: the scan gives no separate hour figure for this item.
Test question: "Who looks at a new applicant first, and how long does it take before they get a reply?"

**4. Where to start (5 min)**
- **Month 1: Pre-screening.** Least preparation: new applicants expect contact, their details are fresh (sidestepping the 55% reachability problem), one WhatsApp template to approve, one decision on which inflow channels count. Also lifts completeness and can capture consent in the same flow. Week one: connect WhatsApp, approve the template, run on new inflow only.
- **Month 2: Ownership cleanup.** No channel needed; needs one assignment rule from them. Then Client reactivation can run.
- **Later: Matching follow-up nudge** on the 6,095 matches, gated on consent and reachability. Add a **GDPR consent** step alongside Pre-screening so the base grows from 46.3%.

**5. Close (3 min)**
Ask: agreement on the first automation and a named owner on their side for the WhatsApp template and the inflow decision. Next step: pilot kick-off date in the next two weeks.

**6. Likely objections, answered from the scan**
- "Our data is too messy." The scan says which part: completeness 37.2%, reachability 55%. Pre-screening is the automation that fills exactly those gaps.
- "Candidates will find it spammy." One personal message, in their consent window, and their own reply updates their own record. Nothing goes to people who have not consented; that is why consent is step one.
- "We tried email before." The projection is hours saved from work already queued, not reply-rate dependent; and the conservative case (6 hrs/wk) is the one to plan on.
- "No time to set this up." They decide three things: channel, one template, one audience rule. The agent configures the rest.
- "Where are the placements?" Honest answer: the scan says none yet, because the database is young. That number changes as matches get followed up and the client list gets owners.
