# Discovery and prioritization

## Discover the problem

Investigate recurring moments where speed, consistency, reach, or follow-up matters:

- Candidate: application acknowledgement, screening, interview preparation and feedback, availability, reactivation, rejection, placement, onboarding, redeployment, and referral.
- Recruiter: missing data, reminders, task creation, handoffs, approvals, exception handling, and daily prioritization.
- Client: vacancy intake, candidate submission, interview feedback, progress updates, placement follow-up, satisfaction, and expansion.

Ask for evidence such as event volume, backlog size, time per case, response time, conversion loss, error rate, complaints, and repeated support requests.

## Score opportunities

Use a 1–5 score for each factor:

| Factor | High score means |
|---|---|
| Reach | Many people or records are affected |
| Frequency | The moment occurs often |
| Outcome value | It improves conversion, experience, speed, quality, or risk |
| Data readiness | Required fields and relationships are reliable |
| Effort | Reverse score: low implementation and maintenance effort |
| Operational safety | Reverse score: low risk of unwanted or incorrect actions |

Use the score to support judgment, not replace it. A lower-volume compliance or safety problem can outrank a high-volume convenience.

## Design a balanced workflow

For every proposed automation, specify:

1. Event or schedule that starts it.
2. Filters that define the intended population.
3. Recipient and sender relationships.
4. Channel and content.
5. Response or measurable signal.
6. Follow-up path for positive, negative, missing, and invalid responses.
7. Owner of exceptions.
8. Metric and review date.

Avoid recommending isolated messages without a downstream action, measurement, or ownership path.
