# Writing the reply

A deliverability diagnosis is usually delivered to someone who suspects HireData broke their
email. The order you present findings in determines whether they believe the analysis.

## Structure

Use this order. It is deliberate.

1. **Thank them and confirm you looked properly.** One line. Name the period you analysed and
   the volume, so it is obvious this is not a brush-off.
2. **Lead with the health baseline.** Delivery rate, complaint rate, unsubscribe rate, bounce
   rate, with the numbers. This removes the suspicion that the platform is at fault before you
   ask them to accept a harder message. If the baseline is *not* healthy, say that here instead
   and the rest of the letter changes shape.
3. **Name the real cause with its evidence.** Usually the frequency table. Show it.
4. **Address their own explanation directly.** They will have offered one — "nothing changed",
   "the content is the same", "it must be your servers". Engage with it explicitly rather than
   talking past it. When "nothing changed" is itself the finding, say so kindly and show why.
5. **Give the sender comparison.** Their own recruiters' open rates against the bulk address.
   This is the exhibit that ends the platform argument, because they can verify it themselves.
6. **Advice in effect order.** Numbered, concrete, and something they can start on Monday.
7. **Own what is ours.** Ramp failures, missing warm-up guidance, limits we set and never
   revisited. Put this near the end, stated plainly, with whether it is resolved.
8. **Ask the question that makes the comparison honest.** How did their previous system
   calculate open rate? Almost nobody knows offhand, and the answer often closes several points
   of the apparent gap.
9. **Offer to sit down together.** End on collaboration, not on a defence.

## Tone

Write in the customer's language. For Dutch customers that means Dutch, including in the
internal draft, so the person sending it does not have to translate a technical argument under
time pressure.

Be direct about numbers and gentle about blame. "Je schrijft dat de opmaak niet is veranderd —
dat klopt, en dat is waarschijnlijk juist de kern" lands; "your content is stale" does not. The
customer made reasonable decisions one at a time and ended up somewhere they did not intend;
the letter should read as though you understand that.

Avoid the word "spam" as a description of their mail. Use "inbox placement", "bulk folder", or
name the provider behaviour directly.

## What not to do

- Do not quote a spam-folder percentage. It does not exist, and claiming one will eventually be
  contradicted.
- Do not present a benchmark as a target. "Typical is 20–35%" is context; "you should be at
  30%" is a promise you cannot keep, because it depends on their list and their offer.
- Do not lead with an upsell. If a paid engagement is right, it belongs after the free advice
  has been given, framed as the next step rather than the price of an answer.
- Do not send recipient email addresses, even in an attachment. Aggregate by domain or provider.
- Do not blame their previous vendor. The comparison question is about measurement definitions,
  not about who was better.

## Worked opening (Dutch)

An anonymised shape to adapt, not to paste:

> Hoi [naam],
>
> Bedankt voor je vraag en voor de cijfers die je meestuurde. We hebben jullie
> e-mailstatistieken van [periode] doorgenomen — in totaal [N] verzonden e-mails. Hieronder wat
> we zien.
>
> **Eerst het goede nieuws: de aflevering is gezond.** Van de [N] e-mails is [X]% afgeleverd.
> Het aantal spamklachten is [Y]% — ongeveer [Z] keer lager dan de grens waarbij providers
> ingrijpen. Uitschrijvingen staan op [A]%, harde bounces op [B]%. Technisch is er dus niets mis
> met de verzending.
>
> **Wat wél veranderd is, is het aantal mails per persoon.** In [maand] ontving een kandidaat
> gemiddeld [N] mail per maand. Vanaf [maand] zijn dat er [M] per maand, en dat is zo gebleven.
> In dezelfde periode daalde de open rate van [X]% naar [Y]%.
>
> Je schrijft dat de opmaak en het verzendmoment niet zijn veranderd. Dat klopt — en dat is
> waarschijnlijk juist de kern. [...]

Then the sender comparison, the numbered advice, what HireData should have done better, and the
question about how their previous system measured opens.
