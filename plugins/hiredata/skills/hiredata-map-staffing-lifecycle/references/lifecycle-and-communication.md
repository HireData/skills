# Match lifecycle, market context, communication, and essential data

Portable defaults. Confirm actual stages, fields, and values against the workspace via the HireData MCP before acting. The live `capabilities` response is the source of truth; a stage or field not listed here is **undiagnosed** (inspect it) rather than something to force into the nearest row below.

## Match stages and advancing events

Match stages are **configurable per workspace**; a common Carerix-style shape runs:

`application → selection → proposal → interview → success / unsuccess → placement`

- **Proposal** means the recruiter proposes the candidate to the contact (hiring manager).
- **Interview** stages precede a success/unsuccess decision.
- **Placement** is created when the company decides to hire.

For any record, identify the stage it is *actually* in and the single event that legitimately advances it. Surface contradictions rather than acting on them — e.g. a match at *unsuccess/Rejected* should not trigger onboarding ("welcome to your first day") or a "role filled" note to the client. If the requested action implies a different stage than the record shows, the stage or the candidate identity is probably wrong; ask.

## Market context changes the right action

| Market | Balance | Recruiter focus | Typical motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Candidate-driven** | More jobs than candidates | Find and secure the right candidates fast | Send **job alerts** to well-matched candidates; move quickly before competitors reach the same people |
| **Job-driven (employer-driven)** | More candidates than jobs | Find jobs; sell strong candidates | Proactively send candidates to contacts ("I have the best ones available") |

In a candidate-driven market, speed and relevance are decisive: competitors chase the same scarce candidates, so the recruiter needs phone numbers ready and enough background to know a candidate is a genuine fit. Sending job alerts to poorly matched candidates backfires — they get annoyed and start ignoring messages.

## The client (company/contact) journey

The candidate journey has a mirror on the client side, and the right audience for each step is the **contact/hiring manager**, not the candidate:

`vacancy intake / briefing → candidate submission (proposal) → interview scheduling & feedback → offer → placement → aftercare / NPS → repeat vacancies`

- **Intake** is where job **requirements** and offered **salary** are captured — get these right and downstream matching improves.
- **Submission** is the proposal stage from the client's side; chasing timely **interview feedback** from the contact is one of the highest-leverage follow-ups (client silence, not candidate quality, kills many matches).
- **Aftercare** (post-placement contact, NPS) is what earns **repeat vacancies** — the same contact is the source of the next job.

## Offer-to-start is fragile

Between offer acceptance and the actual start date, placements fall through — counteroffers from the current employer, competing offers, or simple no-shows. Stay in close contact across this window with the candidate. For permanent/CDI hires this window overlaps the **guarantee / fall-off period**, so early aftercare directly protects the placement and the fee.

## Consent, privacy, and retention

Treat this as context-dependent and flag uncertainty rather than giving legal advice. A person existing in the ATS is **not** blanket consent to contact them for anything or to hold their data indefinitely — purpose, consent basis, and retention limits apply (and are stricter in the EU/GDPR context). When a request assumes "they're in our system, so we can…", surface the purpose/consent question instead of proceeding.

## Communication cadence and anti-patterns

- **Reaching out:** finding candidates or jobs usually means bulk email or WhatsApp. Reach broadly, but do not overdo it — nobody wants to be spammed, and messages flagged as spam damage sender reputation with both customers and the email/WhatsApp provider.
- **Staying in touch:** once the right job and candidate are found, keep contact on a **regular** basis. A good call followed by two weeks of silence loses trust. Cadence depends on the type of job.
- **After phone contact:** add a follow-up — in the agenda, or via an automated WhatsApp/email message.
- **Duration:** contact runs continuously from the moment the candidate/contact is proposed through to placement, and generally continues after placement.
- **Quality signal:** NPS surveys between stages are a normal way agencies measure and improve.

Right audience + right timing + right stage is the test for any outreach. Wrong-stage, wrong-audience, or too-frequent messaging is the recurring anti-pattern.

## Essential data (check first; ask if missing)

**Candidate**
- **Availability** — often the single most decisive field. You cannot propose someone who is not available, and it drives whether to contact them at all.
- Work experience and education — do they fit the role.
- Salary expectation — how much they want to earn.

**Job**
- **Requirements** — licenses (e.g. driver's license), required experience/certifications — so the recruiter can search for the right candidate.
- **Salary** offered — how much the company will pay.

Salary matters on **both** sides and is a common gap. Before acting on a match, confirm availability (candidate) and requirements (job); if a decisive field is missing, ask the one question that changes the decision instead of guessing or inventing it.
